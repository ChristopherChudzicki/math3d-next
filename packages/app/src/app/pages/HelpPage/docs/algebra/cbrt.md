\(x^{1/3}\) gives the same real cube root. A power whose exponent is written as a fraction of whole numbers with an odd denominator, such as \(x^{1/3}\) or \(x^{2/5}\), takes the real root of a negative \(x\). With a decimal or variable exponent that is not a whole number, as in \(x^{0.2}\) or \(x^{a}\), a negative \(x\) gives the complex principal root, which is not drawn.
